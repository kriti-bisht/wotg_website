The Foundation

CapabilitiesWhat human beings need to be able to do.

Broad human capacities that enable people to navigate work and life effectively.

They provide the foundation for the architecture: the enduring human capabilities that matter across roles, industries and situations.

For exampleInterpersonal Responsiveness
What We Develop

ReflectorsHow those capabilities become visible.

Capabilities are broad. They become observable through specific human skills and behaviors.

WOTG calls these Reflectors because they reflect the presence and sophistication of an underlying capability in action. They are precise enough to diagnose, develop and apply in real workplace situations.

For exampleAttuned Listening
Where We Intervene

DriversWhy the skill does — or does not — show up here.

Knowing the skill is not always the problem. The same Reflector may show up strongly in one situation and poorly in another, because different internal and contextual forces are influencing what the person sees, believes, wants, feels able to do, or considers safe.

WOTG calls these forces Drivers. They help explain what is enabling, distorting or blocking effective demonstration of the Reflector in that specific situation.

Why this distinction matters

A visible behavior is not necessarily the source of the problem.

If someone is not listening effectively, the answer may be to improve listening technique.

But it may also be that they are impatient, threatened, overly focused on being right, or simply do not believe the conversation deserves their attention.

The behavior looks the same.
The reason underneath it may be completely different.

What you can seeWhat is underneath

Same behavior. Different cause. Different development.

What learners increasingly expect from workplace learning

Five things people now ask of their learning.

01
Make it relevant to me.

Employees increasingly expect learning to reflect their own needs, context and goals — not simply place everyone into the same program.

02
Let me learn when I need it.

The need to learn often appears in the middle of work: before a difficult conversation, while navigating a new responsibility, or when a familiar approach is simply not working.

Learning should be available when the need is real — not weeks later when the next program happens to run.

03
Don’t make me learn everything.

Time remains one of the biggest constraints on workplace development.

People need enough learning to move forward effectively — without having to work through content they already know or do not need.

04
Give me agency.

Modern workplace learning is increasingly moving toward self-directed development: helping people identify what they need and giving them the flexibility to learn in ways that fit their work and circumstances.

05
Help me use it.

The value of learning is not that somebody completed it.

It is that something useful happens when they return to the work.

03

Learn only what you need.

WOTG does not try to teach everything there is to know about a human skill.

It identifies what is most relevant to the situation.

Using its proprietary diagnostics methodology, it identifies the friction that is getting in the way of demonstrating the required skill in that situation — not just which skill is needed, but what is stopping this person from showing it, in this context.

It then delivers a focused Learning Atom that trains only that.

05

Learning ends in action.

Before leaving the experience, the user makes a simple commitment:

How will I apply this learning?

WOTG then follows up to ask whether they did — and what happened when they tried.

Because the real test of human-skills learning is not what someone knows. It is what they do differently at work.
